Sources and methods

A site about sustainability is only useful if you can trust it. These are our rules.

Last reviewed 22 November 2026.

How we choose sources

  • Every statistic carries a source, a year and a working link.
  • We prefer international agencies (such as the IEA, ITU and WHO), government science bodies, peer-reviewed papers and organisations' own reports, in that order.
  • Where estimates vary, we show a range rather than a single number, and say what the range depends on.
  • Every case study names one limitation, so success stories read as honest rather than promotional.
  • We write in our own words and link out rather than copying long quotes.

How we check them

  • Each lesson and card shows when it was last checked.
  • We test every source link before publishing and review all statistics at least once a year.
  • Images of people come from Wikimedia Commons with their licence and author credited on the page.

Corrections

When we find a mistake we fix it and, for significant ones, say so on the page. Recent corrections:

  • The chainsaw-detection case study was wrongly labelled as Google's Project Loon (an internet-balloon project). It is now correctly credited to Rainforest Connection.
  • A 2023 review paper, "Artificial intelligence-based solutions for climate change: a review", was removed from our reading list because the journal retracted it in 2025.
  • Unsourced figures from earlier versions of the site (such as a claim that a cooling project was "now applied to sustainable AI model training") were removed or replaced with sourced ones.

Spotted an error? Tell us.

How Climate Quest uses its sources

Each card in Climate Quest pairs a problem with a solution and carries one fact with its source. The cards are stored in one open file (data/cards.json), so anyone can check them. Scores are simple and do not claim to measure real emissions:

  • Each correct match earns 2 seeds and 10 points.
  • Each wrong pair costs 1 energy. Energy left at the end adds 2 points per unit.
  • Matching every pair adds a 20-point bonus and introduces a new climate hero.

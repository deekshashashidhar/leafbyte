Green tech basics

Ten ways to cut technology's footprint, each with one thing you can try today.

1. Pick the smallest model that works

Bigger models use more energy for every answer. Smaller or distilled models, such as DistilBERT, often do the job well enough.

Try this today: before reaching for the largest model, test whether a smaller one meets your accuracy target.

2. Fine-tune instead of training from scratch

Reusing a pre-trained model saves most of the compute. LoRA (low-rank adaptation) is a fine-tuning technique that updates only a small number of extra weights.

Try this today: search a model hub for a pre-trained model before starting a new training run.

3. Run on cleaner electricity

The same job emits different amounts depending on the grid that powers it. Cloud regions and times of day vary in how clean their electricity is.

Try this today: check your cloud provider's published carbon data for your region before choosing where to deploy.

4. Make training cheaper

Mixed-precision training, early stopping, pruning and quantisation all cut the compute a model needs, often with little loss in accuracy.

Try this today: turn on early stopping so training ends when the model stops improving.

5. Measure what you use

You cannot cut what you do not count. Open-source tools such as CodeCarbon estimate the emissions of your code as it runs.

Try this today: wrap one training script with an emissions tracker and note the result.

6. Keep hardware longer

Much of a device's footprint comes from making it, and most e-waste is never formally recycled. Repairing and reusing devices avoids both.

Try this today: find out whether your old phone or laptop can be repaired, resold or properly recycled near you.

7. Write efficient code

Efficient algorithms, less data moved over the network and fewer wasted cycles mean less hardware and less power.

Try this today: profile one slow function and fix the biggest bottleneck.

8. Cool data centres smarter

Cooling can use a large share of a data centre's energy. Better airflow, liquid cooling and AI-controlled cooling all help.

Try this today: read the DeepMind cooling case study.

9. Count the whole life cycle

A life-cycle assessment counts manufacturing, use and disposal, not just electricity, so hidden impacts show up.

Try this today: read how the BLOOM team counted theirs.

10. Ask for transparency

Clear reporting of AI's energy and emissions lets researchers, buyers and governments make better choices.

Try this today: when you use an AI service, look for its published sustainability report.
